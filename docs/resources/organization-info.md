Information for Organizations and Advocacy Groups
=================================================

If you belong to an organization or advocacy group active during
the municipal election, there are several ways waterlooregionvotes.org
can cooperate with you to get your message out.

This website is **non-partisan** and does not advocate for any
particular candidate or issue, but we are eager to link to
election-related information on the site, including work done by
advocacy groups. 

Based on previous elections, we predict that several thousand people
will visit waterlooregionvotes.org during the campaign period. Many
will visit the week before the election. If you hold an event, record
a debate, or publish a questionnaire, having us link that material on
your website puts it in a central hub voters will visit as they do
their election research. Posting your efforts to social media is fine,
but often such posts get lost in the stream of content online.


Events
------

If you are organizing an event (a debate, a candidate meet and greet,
an information session...) related to the election please let us know
about it. Provide us with the date and time of your event, which
position(s) it applies to, and a website link where you are
advertising the event. (Make sure that link is public and does not
require a login in order to access.)

Ideally, you should get us information about events you are organizing
at least a week in advance. A longer leadtime is better. 



All-Candidate Meeting Recordings
--------------------------------

If you are organizing an all-candidates meeting or debate, please try
to get the **meeting recorded** and uploaded to a multimedia sharing
platform like YouTube or archive.org . Then send us a link to the
meeting recording.

All-candidates meetings are the **best way** for voters to learn about
their candidates, so we are trying to get as many of them linked on
the website as possible. 

If you are asking questions of candidates, have
the moderator **say the candidate name** before each candidate gives
their answer. This is very useful to those voters who listen to the
audio of all-candidates meetings and do not watch the video. 

If you are running an all-candidates meeting but are unclear how to
record it (or do not have the capacity to do so) then please get in
touch with us; depending on our volunteer availability we may be able
to help.

If you would like to organize an all-candidates meeting but are not
sure of how to go about it, here are some guides: 

- [Your Guide to Hosting an All-Candidates
  Meeting](https://cuc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Your-Guide-to-Hosting-an-All-Candidates-Meeting-.pdf)
  published by the Canadian Unitarian Council: 
  Comprehensive. Not every group will want to follow every
  recommendation, but it is worth being alert to the issues this guide
  raises.
- [Organizing an All Candidates Meeting] published by the Canadian
  Federation of University Women Ontario Council: A concise outline of
  what to organize.

Questionnaires
--------------

Many organizations put out questionnaires on specific issues for
municipal candidates. These are great resources for voters, so we
would like to link to them. 

Ideally, questionnaires should include the **full text of the
responses** given
by candidates. Some advocacy groups choose to interpret those
responses and "grade" the candidates on their responses. That is fine,
but it is very helpful to publish the full text as well.

When possible, it is best to publish questionnaire results as **HTML
pages**, as opposed to PDF or Word documents. This is much easier for
voters to navigate. 

AI search engines also seem to process plain HTML
pages better than PDFs, for some reason. That is important if you want
the results of your questionnaire to be factored into the "AI
Overview" summaries search engines provide these days. Somewhat
unfortunately, many many voters will be asking AI models to help them
choose who to vote for.

Having said that, publishing PDFs is better than not publishing the
results of your questionnaire at all. Do what you can.

Questionnaires are best organized by position (for example, "Regional
Council for Kitchener", "Wilmot Ward 2"), with a **unique link or
section for each position**. This helps voters navigate your
questionnaire more effectively. If every position has a unique link
then we can include those links in specific position pages on our
website.

Please publish the results of your questionnaire on a site that is
publicly-accessible, and does not require an account to access.

As with meeting recordings, if you have questions or would like
assistance in posting your questionnaires in an easy-to-consume
format, please contact us. We will not host your questionnaire, and we
will not promise to do all the work for you, but we may be able to
help in some meaningful ways.


Tell Us About Local Media Items
-----------------

Neighbourhood associations and other organizations often have a good
sense of what is happening locally. If you are aware of debates,
questionnaires, or other media that is being locally produced then
please let us know about them. We can only link what we know about,
and having many people watching out for media items is much more
effective than having only a handful of volunteers trying to watch
everything.


Spread the Word
---------------

Please let your membership and others in your networks know about
waterlooregionvotes.org. If you send out newsletters or email blasts,
please mention us in them. If you hold meetings, please give us a
mention. Ask your membership to spread the word as well. This website
is intended to help all voters in Waterloo Region make informed voting
decisions, and that means spreading the word.
